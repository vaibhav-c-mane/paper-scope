    For example

    Finding research papers: common questions

    Is PaperScope free?

    Yes. Anyone can search, with no account and no sign-up. PaperScope looks through open catalogues of scholarly articles (OpenAlex, arXiv, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar and Crossref) and links each paper to where it is published, and to a free copy when there is one.

    How do I find research papers on a topic?

    Type the topic the way you would say it, such as “making neural networks smaller for phones”. PaperScope fixes typing mistakes, leaves out words like “find me papers on”, and also searches by meaning, so papers that use other words for the same idea still turn up. Each result shows how well it matches your search and which of your words it does not mention.

    How do I find free, open access papers?

    Results with a free copy show Free full text. Open it to read the whole paper without a paywall.

    How can I tell whether a paper is peer reviewed?

    Each result says Peer reviewed when it was published in a journal or at a conference, and Preprint when it is a version shared before review, as on arXiv. Top venue and Strong venue mark highly ranked journals and conferences.

    How do I find the latest research?

    Add “recent” or “latest” to your question to keep papers from the last five years, or set the years above the results. Newest first sorts by date while keeping the papers that match best on top.

    Can I search by DOI or title?

    Yes. Paste a DOI such as 10.1038/nature14539, an arXiv number, or the exact title, and that paper comes first.

    How do I cite a paper?

    Open the paper and choose Copy citation: it copies the authors, year, title, journal and link, ready to paste into your reference list.

    How do I find papers related to one I like?

    Open the paper. Related papers lists the closest ones through citations and similar topics, and Citation links shows the papers it cites and the papers that cite it.

    Papers and their details come from OpenAlex, arXiv, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar and Crossref. Thank you to arXiv for use of its open access interoperability.
